How the health model works
  • Two kinds of wear add up. Time wear grows with heat and with a high charge level. Use wear grows with each charge and discharge, and more with deep ones.
  • Equations and coefficients: Naumann et al., J. Energy Storage 17 (2018) and J. Power Sources 451 (2020), for a lithium iron phosphate cell, checked identical to the authors' open-source code (SimSES, TU Munich).
  • The same equations run for a standard kit in the same home, with the same weather and use. The gap between the two is what SABIL adds.
  • The projection uses this home's data only and assumes the coming years look like the time since installation (wear grows with the square root of time). The official figures are from the 10-year study on 2014–2023 weather: 7.5 years to 80% capacity with SABIL, 6.2 with a standard kit.
  • A second model, NREL's BLAST-Lite for large prismatic LFP cells, gives the same conclusion. This page runs the TU Munich model, which fades faster, so it is the cautious choice. Both were fitted on lab cells, not yet on this module; the pilot logs every battery hourly to check.

How good is the forecast?
  • A machine-learning model (gradient-boosted trees with ridge regression) trained on NASA POWER and ERA5 weather for Maiduguri: cloud, humidity, pressure and wind. It is issued at midnight and corrected during the day with the panels' own readings.
  • Tested year by year on years it never saw (2014 to 2023). Next-day error: 0.61 kWh/m² a day, against 0.80 for "same as yesterday" and 0.64 for the seasonal average. The difference is statistically significant.
  • In the rainy season (June to September) it is no better than the seasonal average (0.97 against 0.97).
  • The low estimate held on 76% of days; the target was 80%.
  • For battery life the forecast matters little: 7.59 years with the model, 7.51 with "same as yesterday" (10-year study, 2014–2023). The rules do most of the work.

What the words mean
kWh
An amount of energy. 1 kWh runs a 20 W fan for 50 hours. A home here uses about 1.2 kWh a day.
W
Power right now. The 400 W of panels give up to about 370 W in full sun.
Charge
How full the battery is, from 0% to 100%.
Battery health
How much the battery can still hold compared with new. Batteries are usually replaced at 80%.
Essentials
Lights, phone charging, the night fan and the fridge. Always served first.
Essentials reserve
Charge kept back so essentials last until the sun is back. Other circuits stop below it.
Forecast range
From the low estimate (P10, a poor day) to the expected value (P50).
Standard kit
The same panels and battery with the usual controller: charge to 100% whenever there is sun, no forecast, no priority.
₦505 per kWh
SABIL's price per kWh from the cost model: it pays for the kit over 20 years with no ongoing subsidy.
